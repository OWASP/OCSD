# Module 1 – Secure Design, Architecture and Threat Modeling

This outline defines the knowledge and skills assessed in Module 1.

## 1.1 Security in the software development lifecycle

- Sources of security requirements, including risks, policies, standards, and applicable obligations
- The role of security requirements during design, implementation, verification, maintenance, and retirement
- The relationship between security requirements, architecture decisions, implementation, and tests
- The review of security assumptions when software, dependencies, or the operating environment change
- The difference between a weakness in the design and a defect in its implementation
- The developer's responsibility to clarify assumptions, implement assigned controls, and report constraints and remaining risks

## 1.2 Security architecture and secure design

- The purpose of security architecture and its relationship to the application's security goals
- Assets and the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and other security properties they require
- Application components, data stores, external services, dependencies, and interfaces
- Data flows, entry points, exit points, and trust boundaries
- Security assumptions and constraints that affect the design
- The placement of controls at trust boundaries and other reliable enforcement points
- The security consequences of component, dependency, interface, and technology choices
- The relationship between the intended architecture and the implemented system
- The purpose and application of these secure design principles:
  - Secure by default
  - Minimize the attack surface
  - Least privilege
  - Separation of duties
  - Defense in depth
  - Complete mediation
  - Fail securely
  - Simple and reviewable design

## 1.3 Threat modeling

- The purpose, timing, participants, and expected outcomes of threat modeling
- The Four Question Framework and the purpose of each question
- The relationships among assets, threats, vulnerabilities, risks, controls, mitigations, and residual risks
- The scope, assumptions, users, components, dependencies, data flows, entry points, and trust boundaries of the system under review
- Data flow diagrams and equivalent ways to represent a system for threat modeling
- STRIDE as a prompt for identifying spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial-of-service, and elevation-of-privilege threats
- The use of application context, likelihood, and impact to prioritize threats
- Risk responses, including avoidance, mitigation, transfer, and acceptance
- The ownership of risk responses and the need for an authorized decision-maker to accept residual risk
- The events that require a threat model to be reviewed or updated

## 1.4 Threat-modeling outputs and security requirements

- The translation of agreed mitigations into specific and testable security requirements
- The connection between security requirements and design decisions, implementation work, backlog items, or acceptance criteria
- Tests that verify required controls and threat mitigations
- Traceability from identified threats to requirements, implementation, tests, and risk decisions
- The recording of assumptions, decisions, action owners, and residual risks
- The tracking of mitigations through completion or an approved risk decision
