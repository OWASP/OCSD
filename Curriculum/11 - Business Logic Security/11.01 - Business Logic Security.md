# Module 11 – Business Logic Security

This outline defines the business logic security knowledge assessed in Module 11.

## 11.1 Business logic flaws

- Business logic as the rules and workflows that define how an application performs its business functions
- Business logic flaws as uses of legitimate features in ways the business did not intend
- Why requests that exploit logic flaws often look valid and are missed by generic scanners and input filters
- Assumptions about user behavior, client behavior, and request order as common sources of logic flaws
- Abuse cases and misuse cases as a way to identify logic flaws from business requirements

## 11.2 Enforcing business rules on the server

- Enforcement of every business rule at a trusted server-side layer
- Client-supplied values that must not be trusted: prices, totals, discounts, quantities, account identifiers, and status fields
- Calculating security-relevant values on the server from authoritative data
- Business constraints on values: ranges, negative numbers, zero, maximum limits, and allowed combinations of fields
- Constraints that depend on time, such as deadlines, opening hours, and validity periods
- Numeric errors that affect business outcomes: integer overflow, rounding, precision, and currency conversion
- Per-user and global limits defined by the business, such as maximum orders, transfers, or redemptions

## 11.3 Workflows and state

- Multi-step workflows, such as checkout, registration, approval, and account recovery
- Skipping, repeating, or reordering steps by calling later steps directly
- Tracking workflow progress on the server and checking the required previous steps before each step
- Valid state transitions for business objects and rejection of transitions that are not allowed
- State or step information supplied by the client as untrusted input
- Binding workflow state to the user and the object it concerns
- Idempotent handling of repeated submissions, such as double clicks and retried requests
- Expiry of incomplete workflows and of reservations, tokens, and other time-limited artifacts
- Multi-person approval for high-value or high-risk operations

## 11.4 Race conditions in business operations

- Race conditions in which concurrent requests change the outcome of an operation
- Time-of-check to time-of-use (TOCTOU) flaws in business operations, such as spending a balance twice or redeeming a single-use coupon several times
- Making check-and-act sequences atomic with database transactions, conditional updates, locks, and unique constraints
- Optimistic concurrency control with version checks
- Idempotency keys for operations that must happen only once
- Why checks in application memory do not protect operations handled by several servers

## 11.5 Abuse of business features

- Discount and coupon abuse: guessable codes, reuse of single-use codes, and combining discounts that should not combine
- Referral, reward, and loyalty program abuse through fake or duplicate accounts
- Reservation and inventory abuse, such as holding stock or seats without completing a purchase
- Refund, cancellation, and chargeback abuse
- Exposure of internal or unfinished functions that bypass normal business rules
- Limits per account, per payment method, and per time period as controls against feature abuse

## 11.6 Automated threats against business flows

- Automated threats to business flows: scalping, scraping, mass account creation, card testing, and credential stuffing
- Why automated abuse can succeed while every individual request is valid
- Rate limits per account, device, and network source, and their limits against distributed attacks
- Challenges such as CAPTCHA, and their effect on usability and accessibility
- Signals such as request timing, behavior patterns, and device characteristics
- Business-level controls, such as purchase limits, verification steps, and delayed fulfillment
- The relationship to resource consumption limits for APIs in Module 13 and to authentication attacks in Module 2

## 11.7 Detecting and responding to abuse

- Logging business events, such as orders, refunds, redemptions, and approvals, with the user and outcome
- Monitoring for unusual patterns, such as high volumes, repeated failures, or values outside normal ranges
- Reconciliation of business records, such as payments, inventory, and balances
- Responding to detected abuse by limiting, reviewing, or reversing affected operations

## 11.8 Testing business logic

- Test cases derived from business rules and abuse cases
- Tests that modify client-supplied values such as prices, quantities, and identifiers
- Tests that skip, repeat, and reorder workflow steps
- Boundary tests with negative numbers, zero, very large values, and unusual combinations
- Concurrent requests that test race conditions in limited or single-use operations
- Tests that exceed business limits and check that automated abuse is limited
- The role of manual review and testing, because automated scanners rarely find logic flaws
